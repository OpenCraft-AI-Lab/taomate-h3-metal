Key results:
1. All observed D−R values were at most 0.703125 MiB. The large gap in this
   diagnostic lies in PyTorch allocator heaps (R−A), rather than a large measured
   allocation outside those heaps. This alone does not identify the allocator's
   exact cause or prove the full model's 31 GiB gap has the same complete cause.

2. One-phase .3 versus .8 preserves the same A peak while reducing R and PID
   peak by about 1.094 GiB. At the matched second-phase layer38 FC1 output point:
     both A = 58.67715 GiB
     LOW .8 R = 73.47656 GiB
     LOW .3 R = 71.01172 GiB
     R reduction = 2.46484 GiB
   This is the appropriate like-for-like memory comparison. Comparing the two
   stopped jobs' final peaks alone would compare different amounts of completed
   work: .8 reached layer38 and .3 reached layer44 of the second phase.

3. Neither two-phase FC1-interleaved job completed within the fixed cap. Both
   crossed the script's sampled 73 GiB driver stop and the independent 75 GiB
   footprint watchdog stopped the process group. Kernel peaks overshot by
   .476/.119 GiB between sampling/termination. The cap was not raised.

4. Removing the FC1 workload and its associated ~0.4 GiB weight/input storage
   allowed the full two-phase cache control to complete. Its final commit had
   A=60.38627, R=61.00781, R−A=.62154 GiB. Thus the cache+linear allocation
   interleaving materially changes heap use in this synthetic controlled setup.
   Releasing all cache/control objects and empty_cache returned A=R=0 in each
   completed case; remaining D was about .688 MiB.
